Microplastics Monitoring at the Bodega Marine Reserve

Microplastics (MPs) are pollutants derived primarily from the breakdown of plastic products that are part of our everyday lives. These tiny particles are ubiquitous and can reach even marine protected areas, which are designed to provide refuge from pollution, habitat degradation, and other human impacts. To better understand how this pervasive pollution affects marine ecosystems, we conduct seasonal sampling at the Bodega Marine Reserve using standardized protocols designed to minimize contamination. We monitor microplastics in seawater, algae, grazing snails, and mussels. Our goal is to build a long-term dataset that will help us understand how microplastics accumulate and move through marine ecosystems across different trophic levels. After one year of sampling, we have discovered interesting seasonal, spatial and matrix-dependent trends.

Effects of malathion in early life stages of white sea urchin

In collaboration with MC Francine DeCastro, I study the interactive effects of the organophosphate pesticide malathion and ultraviolet radiation during early life stages of the white sea urchin Lytechinus pictus. Using techniques such as respirometry and ELISA, we examine how these stressors affect metabolic activity and the expression of proteins involved in cellular repair, even at non-lethal exposure levels.
